01

Read the trip before you read the price

A low price only helps when the plan fits the trip. For Colombia, start with the moments when mobile data matters most: airport transfer, map use, hotel check-in, ride apps, translation, ticket screens, and messages to people waiting for you. If those moments fall on long travel days, choose validity and data margin before comparing small price differences.

A plan that ends a day early, or one that empties on the busiest day, often costs more in the end than one sized correctly from the start, because you buy a second plan to cover the gap.

Validity is counted in days, so count arrival and departure days as well as the nights in between. A flight that lands late or leaves early can touch more calendar dates than the stay itself, and a plan that ends the morning of departure has ended a day early.

02

Setup timing for Colombia

Install the eSIM profile on steady Wi-Fi before the trip gets busy. Keep the QR email available offline, leave your primary SIM in place if you need calls or bank messages, and select the travel eSIM only for mobile data when you are ready to use it. If data does not register right away, restart the phone and check roaming on the eSIM line before changing advanced settings.

Two things make installation go smoothly: a stable Wi-Fi connection and the QR email saved somewhere you can open without mobile data. Everything else, including switching the eSIM on, can wait until you land.

Keep large app updates, cloud backups and long uploads for Wi-Fi. They are the quiet drain on any allowance, because phones start them the moment they see a connection.

  • Save the QR email somewhere you can open without mobile data.
  • Check eSIM support and carrier unlock status before purchase.
  • Count arrival and departure days in the validity window.
  • Use Wi-Fi for app updates, cloud backups, and long video calls.
03

What to download before weak-signal moments

Before checkout, confirm that the phone is unlocked and supports eSIM, that the plan covers Colombia, and that the validity window includes arrival and departure days. Hotspot sharing, video calls, cloud backups, and laptop work all change the right allowance. A smaller plan can be enough for maps and messaging, but it needs to match the real route.

If you will share your connection on most days, a larger or daily allowance fits better than a small fixed one. If sharing is an occasional need, a smaller plan with room to top up is usually enough.

Size the data from what you do, not from how long you stay. Maps, messaging and browsing use little. Video calls, uploads and hotspot sharing use a lot, and they tend to fall on the same days. Start from the busiest day you can picture and check that the plan covers it.

04

Plan selection notes for this article scenario

The cards below are easier to compare when you sort them by use case. Standard plans suit fixed trips with predictable data use. Daily or flexible options make more sense when the itinerary changes, when several heavy-use days are likely, or when you want more room for navigation and uploads. Open the live product page before buying because plan terms can change.

A useful way to estimate is to list the apps you will rely on and ask which of them you would miss most. Navigation and ride apps run for hours on travel days, while messaging barely registers.

Sharing a connection changes the arithmetic. Every device connected through your phone draws on the same allowance, including operating system updates and sync you did not ask for. Whether sharing is allowed at all depends on the plan's hotspot terms, which are listed on the plan page.

Validity comes in steps, and trips do not. A trip of 8 days is one day longer than the 7-day plans, so the next fit is the 15-day plans. Counting arrival and departure days decides which step you need.

On the daily side, ten days at the smallest allowance (500MB per day) comes to 29.90 USD, and ten days at the largest (3GB per day) to 96.90 USD, if a daily plan is bought for each day.

Quick answers

Which plan detail matters most for travel days?
Validity. A plan has to cover the arrival day, the transfers and the departure day. Data size and price come after.
How cheaply can I get data for Colombia?
The lowest listed price here is 2.99 USD, for Colombia eSIM 500MB/Day.
Does my phone need to be unlocked?
Yes. It must support eSIM and be unlocked by your home carrier. Check before you fly.
Which Colombia plan lasts the longest?
Colombia eSIM 20GB 30 Days at 66.99 USD, with 20GB over 30 days.
Can I buy eSIMs for my travel group in one order?
Yes. eSIMCircle lets you buy several eSIMs in one purchase.
Can the home SIM stay active?
Yes. Many travelers keep it for calls and SMS and use the eSIM for data.
Do arrival and departure days count towards validity?
Yes. Count both in the validity window, especially if transfers stretch across long travel days.
